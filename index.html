Page 107 Let us discuss • Section I

Fact vs. Opinion: Identifying Truths & Beliefs

Key Concept from Page 107:
  • Fact: Something that can be proven true or false based on objective evidence. It does not depend on personal beliefs.
  • Opinion: A belief, feeling, or personal judgment that varies from person to person and cannot be objectively proven.
1
Fact

Encyclopedia’s father was the Chief of Police.

Answer: FACT (Textbook Example)

Explanation: This is an objective, verifiable reality. Chief Brown officially held the post of Chief of Police in the town of Idaville. It is a matter of administrative record, not an opinion or personal belief.

2
Opinion

Everyone thought that Chief Brown must be the smartest police chief in the country.

Answer: OPINION

Explanation: The statement describes what people "thought" (their subjective impressions and beliefs). Whether he is truly the "smartest" cannot be measured or proven as an undeniable fact, especially since it was secretly his son who was cracking the hardest cases.

3
Fact

An encyclopedia is a book or set of books filled with facts from A to Z.

Answer: FACT

Explanation: This is the universal, objective dictionary definition of the reference book known as an encyclopedia. It can be independently verified in any lexicon or library catalog.

4
Opinion

Leroy’s friends said that he was like a library and computer rolled into one, and more user-friendly.

Answer: OPINION

Explanation: This is a creative, metaphorical comparison expressing his friends' personal admiration and subjective viewpoint. Comparing a human to a computer or library is figurative commentary rather than an empirical scientific fact.

5
Fact

Two masked men held up the Diamond Mart on Sixth Avenue.

Answer: FACT

Explanation: This records an actual physical crime and historical event that occurred in the story. It is a documented police case incident that took place at a specific location.

6
Fact

Nolan and Davenport had met while both were in prison in South Carolina.

Answer: FACT

Explanation: This is an objective historical fact supported by prison and correctional records showing both individuals served sentences simultaneously at the same facility in South Carolina.

7
Opinion

Chief Brown’s hunch was that Davenport and Nolan decided to hide the loot until things cooled down.

Answer: OPINION

Explanation: A "hunch" is by definition an intuitive feeling, conjecture, or belief rather than conclusive evidence. Even though his hunch turned out to be accurate, at the time it was merely Chief Brown's subjective opinion/theory.

8
Fact

Nolan wrote a four words code to tell Davenport where he had hidden the stolen jewellery.

Answer: FACT

Explanation: This is a tangible, verifiable truth. The calendar sheet physically existed with the four encoded words (Nom Utes Sweden Hurts) written by Nolan and recovered by police.

Extract Analysis & Deductive Reasoning

1 (i)
Extract 1 • Completion

Complete the following sentence with a suitable reason: Chief Brown’s dilemma can be called unique because ____________.

Completed Sentence: Chief Brown’s dilemma can be called unique because he was immensely proud of his son being the sharpest detective alive, yet he could not tell anyone because nobody would ever believe that an eighth-grade boy was solving complex police cases, and doing so would also compromise Leroy's desire to live as a normal boy among his peers.

In-Depth Explanation: Most parents love bragging about their children's achievements. Chief Brown, however, was caught in an unusual position: while his son's brilliance solved the town's most baffling mysteries, sharing the truth would invite public skepticism, media scrutiny, and unwanted attention for his young son.

1 (ii)
Extract 1 • MCQ

What can be inferred about why Encyclopedia never spoke of the help he gave his father?

A. He is shy and lacks confidence in his abilities to share his insights.
B. He wants to avoid the jealousy others might feel about his skills.
C. He desires to blend with the other boys to prevent seeming different. ✓
D. He is embarrassed about being smarter than most adults around him.
Correct Answer: Option C

Textual Reference: The story plainly states: "He didn’t want to seem different from other boys." Leroy valued normal friendships and fitting in with schoolmates rather than being singled out as an eccentric prodigy.

1 (iii)
Extract 1 • Identification

The parents choose not to call their son ‘Encyclopedia’. Select the reason that is false.

A. For them he is more than his unique abilities; he is their child, with a personal identity.
B. Calling him by his real name reflects their affection and the desire to acknowledge him as an ordinary boy.
C. They want him to be remembered as different from the others around. ✓ (FALSE STATEMENT)
False Statement: Option C

Explanation: Statements A and B are true: his parents call him Leroy because they love him for who he is as their child, not as a novelty. Statement C is completely false because his parents did not want to make him appear different or alienated from others.

1 (iv)
Extract 1 • Vocabulary Meaning

What does the writer mean by the fact that Leroy was ‘stuck with’ the name Encyclopedia?

The phrase "stuck with" means that the nickname "Encyclopedia" had become so ingrained, universal, and widely adopted by everyone in town that Leroy had no power to change, remove, or escape it, regardless of his personal preference.

Nuance: It highlights a gentle irony: despite his extraordinary intelligence and ability to solve difficult crimes, he was powerless against a childhood nickname bestowed on him because of his insatiable reading habits and infallible memory.

Let us think and reflect • Extract 2 (Pages 109–110)
"Chief Brown studied the four words: Nom Utes Sweden Hurts. He shook his head and passed the notebook to Mrs. Brown again. “Can you figure it out?”

“Nom is a shortening of nominative, a grammatical term,” stated Mrs. Brown, who had taught English and other subjects in high school. “Utes is an American Indian tribe. Sweden is a country in northern Europe. Hurts is hurts.”

She lifted her gaze to Encyclopedia and shook her head.

“I can’t figure it out,” she confessed.

“Davenport disappeared right after the hold-up,” Encyclopedia reminded her."
2 (i)
Extract 2 • MCQ

What does Chief Brown’s reaction of shaking his head suggest about his understanding of the four words?

A. He is confident that the words are a clue.
B. He is confused, unable to make sense of the words. ✓
C. He believes the words are random and meaningless.
D. He thinks the words are unrelated to the case.
Correct Answer: Option B

Explanation: Shaking his head while passing the notebook over and asking his wife "Can you figure it out?" demonstrates complete confusion and perplexity. He knew it was significant, but could not decipher its hidden meaning.

Identify whether the given statement is true or false: Mrs. Brown explained the actual meanings of the individual words rather than decoding the hidden message behind them.

What does Mrs. Brown’s inability to decode the words suggest about the message?

It suggests that the message was a secret cipher created for an accomplice, based on practical context (the desk calendar) rather than conventional dictionary definitions or grammar.

Section II • Page 110

Detailed Answer Questions (Questions 1 to 7)

Concise, point-wise model answers tailored for exams and revision.

1
Detailed Answer

Nolan and Davenport were very close. Support this statement with evidence from the text.

The text provides clear evidence of their close bond and mutual trust:

  • Past Companions: They met and formed a friendship while serving time together in a South Carolina prison.
  • Partners in Crime: They jointly carried out the Diamond Mart robbery on Sixth Avenue.
  • Trust with the Loot: Davenport trusted Nolan completely to hide and keep the stolen diamonds safe while he was in hiding.
  • Sole Beneficiary: When Nolan was dying, he left his palm-tree nursery to Davenport in his will along with a secret coded note to locate the hidden jewels.
2
Detailed Answer

Why did Davenport disappear right after the jewellery hold-up? What might his plans have been if, Nolan had not died?

  • Why Davenport disappeared: He fled immediately after the robbery to escape police arrest and lie low until the investigation cooled down.
  • His plans if Nolan had not died: He would have reconnected with Nolan once the police stopped searching, retrieved the hidden diamonds from the nursery, and split the profits safely.
3
Detailed Answer

What does Mrs. Brown’s interest in the case tell us about her?

  • Sharp & Cultured: As a former high school English teacher, she enjoys word puzzles and intellectual problem-solving.
  • Supportive Wife: She takes an active interest in her husband's police work, discussing cases at dinner to help him unwind and brainstorm.
  • Encouraging Mother: She participates in case discussions to provide a gentle, loving platform for Leroy to exercise his brilliant mind.
4
Detailed Answer

Explain why Chief Brown was proud of his son.

  • Extraordinary Detective Mind: Leroy had an encyclopedic memory, never forgot a fact, and could solve cases that baffled full-grown police officers, usually with just one question before dessert.
  • Humility & Modesty: Despite his genius, Leroy never showed off, never bragged to friends, and preferred to remain an ordinary, respectful eighth-grade boy.
5
Detailed Answer

Why were Chief Brown’s suspicions regarding Nolan and Davenport justified, even when there was a lack of concrete proof?

Because of 4 key clues:

  1. A clerk recognized Nolan when his mask slipped.
  2. Nolan and Davenport were known prison partners.
  3. Davenport fled immediately after the robbery.
  4. Nolan left his nursery and a secret coded note to Davenport in his will.
6
Detailed Answer

Analyse the role of the four-word coded message in the story.

  • Connects the Mystery: Links Nolan's death to the 5-year-old unsolved diamond robbery.
  • Contrasts Thinking: Shows that simple calendar context solved what dictionary definitions couldn't.
  • Reveals the Loot: Decodes Mon, Tue, Wed, Thu to the 5th day, Friday (FIR), pinpointing the fir tree hiding place.
7
Character Sketch

Write a character sketch on Leroy with the help of the character traits table given in ‘Let us discuss’ section.

(You may begin it like this: Encyclopedia was a knowledgeable eighth grader who never forgot a fact that he read.)

Knowledgeable Humble Keen Listener Cleverness

Encyclopedia was a knowledgeable eighth grader who never forgot a fact that he read.

  • Knowledgeable: Read widely and remembered every detail, making his mind function like a walking library.
  • Humble: Never bragged about helping his father solve crimes; just wanted to blend in as an ordinary boy.
  • Keen Listener: Sat quietly at the dinner table, patiently absorbing all case facts before speaking.
  • Cleverness: Solved baffling mysteries in minutes with simple logic—such as cracking Nolan's calendar code to find the hidden diamonds.

"The Case of the Fifth Word" adapted from Donald J. Sobol's famous Encyclopedia Brown detective series.

Idaville, USA. Leroy (Encyclopedia) Brown, Chief Brown (Police Chief), Mrs. Brown (former teacher), Tim Nolan (thief), Davenport (accomplice).
